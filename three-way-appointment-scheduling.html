Three way scheduling: the call that ends with an appointment

By Chau Doan, Manager, CareGap Connect · November 26, 2026

The most common ending to a care gap call is also the least useful one: the member agrees the visit matters and promises to call the doctor's office. Life happens. The office line is busy, the hold is long, the member is at work, and the promise quietly expires. The gap stays open and everyone involved meant well.

What three way scheduling does differently

In a three way call, the agent reaches the member first, confirms the gap in plain words, and then brings the provider's office onto the same call. The member does not have to dial anything, repeat anything, or navigate a phone tree. The office hears the member, checks the schedule, and offers real times. The member picks one. The appointment exists before anyone hangs up.

Why it outperforms the referral promise

  • No second call for the member to make. The step where most bookings die is removed entirely.
  • Real availability, not guesswork. The office offers its actual open slots, including the ones held for plan members, instead of the member guessing when to call back.
  • Transportation and language get solved on the same call. If the member needs a ride benefit or an interpreter at the visit, it is arranged while everyone is still on the line.
  • The booking is documented the moment it happens: date, time, office, and who confirmed it, ready for the plan's records and for reminder follow up.

Where it matters most

Three way scheduling earns its keep on the bookable gaps: diabetes eye exams, mammograms, follow up visits for blood pressure rechecks, and annual wellness visits. These are gaps where the member usually already agrees the care is a good idea. The obstacle was never willingness. It was logistics, and logistics is what the second line on the call removes.

A booked appointment is not a closed gap yet. The member still has to keep it, and the proof still has to travel back to the plan. But a season built on real bookings behaves very differently from a season built on promises, and it starts with refusing to hang up before the visit exists.
